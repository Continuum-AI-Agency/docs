## Brand grounding and source support

Your brand kit supplies logos, palette and typography; the brand book supplies creative context. Those inputs do not add support for a source application, install a missing font, or make an unsupported feature editable. Inspect the exact template contract before mapping values.

**Upload → parse → edit → preview → render → export are separate capabilities.** Library acceptance, a thumbnail, a schema enum or a successful parse establishes only that stage. A preview may be a sidecar or a preflight rather than final pixels. No new customer rendering or fidelity qualification was performed for this map (checked 2026-10-29).

## Route by returned identity

- **Published render template:** discover `templateKey`, `bindingId` where needed, variable keys and outputs. Use sets and the render-preview/confirm sequence.
- **Revision source:** keep `assetId`, `variantId` and `revisionId` distinct. Inspect its layers; save immutable revisions rather than replacing the Original. Publication requires an eligible source and appropriate role.
- **Native HTML, component or Blender source:** follow its own exposed contract. Never send it into `template_forge` just because it parsed.
- **Scene:** use the `assetId`, exact `versionId` and output IDs returned by `scenes`, then `scene_fill` and `scene_render`.
- **CAD or another adjacent tool:** load that tool's current guide. Forge identifiers, editing rules and status readers do not transfer automatically.

A ZIP's contents decide its family. A stored original is not proof that an editable export or another application's native project can be generated. Ask for only the outputs the discovered contract declares.

## Before a render

Check fonts, field kinds, required values, text budgets, media/version references, output IDs and the saved delivery settings. Preserve any refusal. Brand-book grounding cannot override a missing dependency or renderer limitation.
